## Overview

Lucky Arduino is the public collection I created when I taught myself electronics.
Across more than fifty videos and twelve documented builds, I worked through
sensors, displays, controls, communication, and electromechanical systems instead
of treating each experiment as an isolated exercise. The idea was that the Youtube channel 
and website acted as an electronics portfolio for me, and while I don't update my channel
frequently anymore, I still have more recent major projects on there such as SmartLift Sleeve and BackBuddy!

## Learning in public

I separated the work into introductory and more substantial project repositories,
then used the website and YouTube channel to explain what I was building. That
public record made the collection both a learning process and an educational
resource I could improve over time.

## Technical range

The projects used Arduino and C/C++ alongside sensors, servos, displays, infrared
control, and serial communication. I also maintained the HTML and CSS surrounding
the project documentation, so presenting a build clearly became part of finishing
it. Lucky Arduino was fully created without the use of AI!

## Evidence

The channel reached more than fifty thousand views and more than two
hundred subscribers. The repo collection includes six introductory and six substantial
documented builds, plus more than fifty instructional and project videos.

## Takeaway

Lucky Arduino became my foundation for later embedded work. Repeating the full
cycle—build, debug, explain, and publish—forced me to understand each circuit well
enough to make it useful to someone else. It also helped me explain myself more clearly
and concisely, so that tutorials were actually useful to other people rather than being too wordy.
