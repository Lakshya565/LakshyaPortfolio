## Overview

BackBuddy is an electronic posture brace I designed and built in a team of 3 as part of Engineering Design and Development (PLTW Engineering Capstone). The
device used a custom circuit and PCB to detect a hunched posture and respond with
vibration, turning posture feedback into something immediate and wearable.

<Callout>
  The design constraint was physical rather than visual: the wearer should not need to check a screen to understand the warning.
</Callout>

## Hardware approach

That constraint decided the build. The sensing, circuit, PCB, and vibration
response all had to behave as one compact hardware system. It was fairly simple - 
a power source with a on/off switch connected to a tilt ball switch, which triggered three vibration motors in parallel when the user's back was hunched. 
Figuring out that circuit wasn't the hard part, it was making sure that the vibration didn't ruin the rest of the circuit
and testing on a wide variety of people to figure out the optimal setup and placement of the PCB.

## Takeaways

BackBuddy earned second place for engineering and product presentation among more
than thirty capstone projects. That result reflects both the working hardware and
the ability to explain why the feedback loop mattered to a potential wearer. I also learned
the basics of circuit design and applying it to create a marketable product.
