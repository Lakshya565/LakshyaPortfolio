## Overview

QuackTA was built by a five-person team during UIUC IEEE's March 28–29, 2026
Bit-by-Bit Hardware Hackathon. The prompt: design a product that integrates hardware and 
software and solves a problem an ECE student at UIUC regularly faces.
Inspired by rubber-duck debugging (the idea of explaining your code and methodology to 
a rubber duck or other similar figure to figure out whether your reasoning and thought process
is correct), it turned the
passive act of explaining a problem into an interactive physical teaching
assistant for ECE coursework and schedule questions. The idea was that we wanted
it to act as a TA for anything ECE-related.

<Callout>
  A tutor that answers the question is not a tutor. The harness around GPT-4o mini exists to keep it guiding a struggling student rather than handing over the solution.
</Callout>

## Technical approach

A Raspberry Pi 5 coordinated the application experience, while an Arduino Uno
R4 Minima drove physical expressions inside a custom 3D-printed duck enclosure.
The tutoring flow used GPT-4o mini with UIUC ECE course and schedule context to
guide a student toward an answer instead of immediately revealing it. We used Flutter
to create a mockup of an IOS-based application, allowing full control of QuackTA from 
the user's phone.

The device also borrowed from Tamagotchi-style feedback. Wing movement, eye
color, and eyebrow position gave the assistant a visible emotional state, so
the enclosure communicated system behavior rather than serving as decoration.

## Takeaways

The strongest part of QuackTA was the integration of physical expression,
contextual guidance, and scheduling into one coherent object. The AI model was
one subsystem; the interaction design depended equally on embedded control,
mechanical presentation, and the surrounding application. I also learned the importance
of time management and working under major pressure and constraints - hackathons are quite
difficult, and even though this was my first one, I feel that this project was one of my best
ones so far given the time and quality constraints!
