## Overview

SmartLift Sleeve was a three-person ECE 145 project at UIUC exploring whether a
wearable could make repetition count and workout intensity visible during a set
without depending on an app, smartwatch, or microcontroller. The semester ended
with a hardware proof of concept rather than a fully realized wearable MVP.

<Callout>
  One caveat introduced by ECE 145 - the design had to intentionally use hardware logic rather than firmware: no app, no smartwatch, and no microcontroller between the lift and the number on the display.
</Callout>

## Problem

Checking a phone or interpreting a raw heart-rate value interrupts a lift. We instead designed two immediate outputs: a two-digit repetition count and a
low, medium, or high exertion signal that could be understood at a glance.

## Technical approach

A flex sensor detects elbow motion during lifts such as curls and bench press.
Analog conditioning and digital counter logic translate that movement into the
two-digit seven-segment display. In parallel, a pulse sensor that the user wears on their earlobe feeds an LED array
that represents one of three exertion levels.

## Contributions

I led development and focused on the analog and digital logic: flex-sensor rep
detection, signal conditioning, timing, counter logic, display output, and LED
intensity feedback. I also helped design the PCB for the output subsystem.
Integration required diagnosing sensor behavior, display faults, power issues,
and interactions between circuits that worked separately but failed together.

## Status and next steps

The project demonstrated its major circuit paths, but we were not able to finish
the complete sleeve during the semester. The documented next steps were to
fabricate the output PCB, extend the PCB across the remaining circuitry, build
the wearable enclosure, and test the system with athletes in a gym.

## Takeaways

Physical systems expose assumptions quickly. Sensor noise, power distribution,
timing, and mechanical placement all changed what looked straightforward in a
block diagram, making integration and honest scope control as important as the
individual circuits. We spent hours debugging small, frankly stupid, errors that
could've been avoided easily. From this, we all learned the importance of patience
and taking the time to check over everything before moving onto the next part and making
the project harder to debug.
